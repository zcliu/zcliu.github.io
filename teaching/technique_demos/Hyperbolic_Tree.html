The animal kingdom on a hyperbolic disk

The same 183 taxa as the degree-of-interest demo, laid out in hyperbolic space and projected onto a disk. Whatever sits at the centre is large and labelled; everything else shrinks smoothly toward the rim, but the whole tree is always on screen. Click a node to slide it to the centre, or drag to move through the tree.

How it works

Every parent–child edge has the same length in the hyperbolic plane, and each node's children share the wedge of space facing away from its parent. Hyperbolic space has room for a tree that branches without limit, because its area grows exponentially with distance.

The plane is drawn on the Poincaré disk: equal hyperbolic distances look shorter the closer they are to the rim, and edges become arcs that meet the rim at right angles.

Moving the focus doesn't redo the layout. It applies a Möbius transformation, a rigid motion of hyperbolic space, so the tree keeps its shape and only your viewpoint changes.
