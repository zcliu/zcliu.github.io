How Reingold–Tilford lays out a tree

The algorithm draws each subtree by itself, then pushes sibling subtrees together until their facing outlines (their contours) are at least one unit apart on every level. Step through it on a lopsided 15-node example.

Pseudocode
  1. layout(v):
  2. for each child c of v: layout(c)
  3. if v is a leaf: return
  4. place first child at offset 0
  5. for each next child c:
  6. put c 1 unit right of prev sibling
  7. walk contours down, level by level
  8. shift c right by the worst overlap
  9. center v over first and last child
  10. second pass, from the root down:
  11. x(c) = x(parent) + offset(c)

What the layout guarantees

  • Nodes at the same depth share one horizontal line.
  • Children keep their left-to-right order.
  • Each parent is centered over its first and last child.
  • Nodes on a level are at least 1 unit apart.
  • A subtree is drawn the same way wherever it appears.
